While steering clear of these foods is essential, focusing on what you _should_ eat plays an equally important role in ensuring your teeth and gums stay healthy throughout your treatment with Dr. Sarina Harman-Tinnel, Dr. Whitney, and Dr. Garling.

### Building Blocks for a Strong Smile

Your teeth and jawbone are living tissues that thrive on proper nutrition. **Calcium** is a cornerstone mineral for maintaining strong enamel and bone density. You’ll find it in dairy products like milk and cheese, as well as in fortified plant-based milks, leafy greens such as spinach and kale, and nuts like almonds.

But calcium works best alongside **phosphorus**, which aids in repairing and reinforcing tooth enamel. Good sources include lean meats, fish, eggs, and beans. To maximize calcium absorption, your body also needs sufficient **Vitamin D**. While Overland Park’s sunny days offer a natural boost, incorporating Vitamin D-rich foods like salmon and fortified cereals can make a difference.

Don’t forget **Vitamin C** — vital for healthy gums and the collagen that supports your teeth. A diet rich in citrus fruits, strawberries, bell peppers, and broccoli helps keep your gum tissue strong and less prone to inflammation.

### Foods That Can Challenge Orthodontic Health

It’s equally important to recognize what can undermine your progress. Excess **sugar** feeds bacteria that produce acids, gradually wearing down enamel and increasing the risk of cavities—something to be mindful of especially when cleaning around braces or aligners is more difficult.

Acidic drinks such as sodas, sports beverages, and certain fruit juices can erode enamel directly, regardless of their sugar content. This acid erosion weakens teeth, making them sensitive and vulnerable. To protect your smile, prioritize drinking water—especially fluoridated water—which helps strengthen enamel and keeps your mouth hydrated.
